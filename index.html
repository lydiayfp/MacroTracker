My honest read, not just a summary of the consensus: the evidence doesn't support chasing pure growth right now, but it doesn't support fleeing to pure defensive either — it supports a barbell. Three separate facts point the same way: (1) since February, industrials/consumer-staples/energy have already been outperforming tech on cost-conscious spending and AI-adjacent capex, not just as a defensive trade; (2) BlackRock's own data shows both cyclicals and defensives beating tech YTD — an unusual pairing that signals broad rotation away from single-factor tech concentration, not a rotation into one specific style; (3) this week's Brent-driven inflation risk is a fresh, near-term reason to hold energy and quality defensives (healthcare, staples, utilities) as ballast, layered on top of that existing trend. None of this means AI earnings momentum has broken — it hasn't. It means the reward for concentration risk has fallen. For a Global Diversified ETF, the actionable version isn't "sell growth, buy defensive" — it's checking whether your fund's sector weights are still tech-heavy from the 2023–25 AI run, and if so, treating any near-term dip as a chance to let the fund's own rebalancing (or your top-ups) drift toward its stated benchmark weights rather than fighting it.
